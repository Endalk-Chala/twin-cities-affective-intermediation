# Job-Application Framing: Strategic Communication

## Core positioning

For faculty applications in Strategic Communication, Public Relations, or related areas, this project should be presented not simply as a dataset but as a **reproducible research infrastructure for studying organizational communication under crisis, uncertainty, and heightened public attention**.

The project connects theory, organizational communication, digital methods, longitudinal data, and reproducible research practice.

## One-sentence version

I am building a reproducible research infrastructure for studying how nonprofit and community organizations communicate through crisis, linking organizational position, platform, time, emotional framing, communication function, calls to action, and visible public uptake in a longitudinal Twin Cities corpus.

## Short cover-letter version

My current strategic communication research examines how nonprofit and community organizations communicate during periods of crisis and uncertainty. In *Affective Intermediation in Twin Cities Nonprofit Communication*, I built a reproducible longitudinal research infrastructure that currently contains 337 verified and coded communication items from 44 organizations. The project links organizational type, platform, temporal context, situational appraisal, emotional framing, communication function, action orientation, and visible public uptake. A scripted validation pipeline rebuilds the analysis-ready corpus, preserves provenance, flags recurring communication series, separates unresolved platform records from verified observations, and documents missingness rather than treating unavailable engagement as zero. This infrastructure supports comparative research on crisis communication, relationship management, public-interest communication, mobilization, and organizational adaptation over time.

## Research-statement version

A second strand of my research examines strategic communication by nonprofit, community, and public-interest organizations. My current project, *Affective Intermediation in Twin Cities Nonprofit Communication*, develops a longitudinal and reproducible infrastructure for studying how organizations interpret crises and translate those interpretations into information, reassurance, advocacy, solidarity, service provision, and mobilization. The validated primary corpus contains 337 coded communication items from 44 organizations across November 2025 through March 2026. The design connects organizational role, communication platform, temporal context, situational appraisal, emotion, communication function, action orientation, and visible public uptake. Rather than relying on a manually assembled final spreadsheet, the project uses a scripted build and GitHub Actions validation workflow that harmonizes coding batches, preserves source identifiers, resolves documented legacy collisions, flags recurring content series, and generates a reproducibility report. The infrastructure provides a basis for comparing service providers, advocacy organizations, faith-based organizations, and community-rooted groups across stages of a crisis and for examining how strategic messages are taken up, recirculated, contested, or converted into observable forms of action.

## Teaching connection

The infrastructure can be used to demonstrate how research and teaching reinforce one another. It can support assignments or modules in:

- strategic communication research methods;
- public relations and nonprofit communication;
- message design and audience analysis;
- crisis and risk communication;
- digital and social media communication;
- computational content analysis;
- ethical use of public digital data;
- reproducible research and data provenance.

A useful teaching formulation is:

> I want students to see strategic communication research as more than coding messages after the fact. They should understand how a research question becomes a sampling frame, how public communication becomes structured data, how coding decisions are documented, how missingness affects claims, and how a transparent workflow allows another researcher to reproduce the analysis.

## What this demonstrates to a search committee

The project provides concrete evidence of the ability to:

1. develop an original theoretically driven research program in strategic communication;
2. design and manage longitudinal organizational communication data;
3. combine qualitative judgment with structured quantitative coding;
4. build reproducible computational research workflows;
5. connect organizational messages to publics, relationships, and action;
6. conduct research on nonprofit, community, advocacy, faith-based, and service organizations;
7. work with web and social-media communication while documenting platform limitations;
8. translate research infrastructure into student methods training and collaborative projects;
9. maintain a public-facing scholarly repository with transparent provenance and validation.

## Claims to avoid

Do not describe the social-media portion as a comprehensive social-media census. Historical social observability is uneven, and the validated primary corpus is substantially website-heavy.

Do not describe engagement counts as measures of emotion. Public uptake, circulation, and affective meaning remain analytically distinct.

Do not call the original AI coding pilot an inter-coder reliability study unless it is compared with an independent human coder.

Do not claim that the infrastructure proves causal effects of organizational communication. It supports longitudinal and comparative analysis of communication patterns, uptake, and observable action signals.

## Strongest broader narrative

The project can sit inside a broader scholarly trajectory:

**platform governance and digital infrastructures → multilingual communication and information integrity → organizational and nonprofit strategic communication → public uptake, trust, and action.**

This trajectory shows continuity rather than a field change: the common question is how institutions and communication systems shape what publics can understand, trust, contest, and act upon.
